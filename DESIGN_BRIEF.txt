THE LOST EXPLORER — DESIGN BRIEF
================================================================================
You are redesigning the entire visual layer of a finished, working web game.

The engine is DONE and TESTED (295 passing tests). Do not rebuild it. Your job is
the look and feel only. This document tells you exactly what you may change, what
you must not touch, and the handful of rules that look like styling choices but
are actually game rules.

Read all of it before you start. The section "SEVEN RULES THAT ARE NOT STYLING"
is the part people get wrong.

================================================================================
1. WHAT THE GAME IS
================================================================================

The Lost Explorer is a daily geography deduction game. Wordle's ritual, but you
are working out WHERE you are rather than guessing a word.

One mystery city per day. Everyone in the world gets the same city. It resets at
00:00 UTC.

The conceit: you are an explorer who has woken up lost. Each "Day" you discover
one more thing about where you are. Five Days maximum.

  Day 1  Surroundings   landscape + population of the urban area
  Day 2  A road sign    "<FAMOUS CITY> is about N km away"
  Day 3  A flag         which colours it had (not the design)
  Day 4  An old map     which historical state this place belonged to, and when
  Day 5  A voice        someone asks "do you need help?" in the local language

The clue ladder moves deliberately through:
  physical geography -> relative geography -> national identity ->
  historical geography -> language

After each Day the player chooses GUESS or CONTINUE. One guess per Day, five
maximum. Guessing wrong advances the Day AND leaves a permanent mark. Continuing
advances the Day with a clean record. That tension is the whole game.

On Day 5 there is no CONTINUE. Only a final guess.

The score people care about is "average Day solved", not speed. A Day 1 solve is
extraordinary. Day 3 is the intended average.

Tone: the explorer is conversational and lightly wry, never florid. A player
should absorb a clue in a few seconds. The repetition is meant to feel familiar
and comforting, like a ritual.

================================================================================
2. CURRENT AESTHETIC — AND YOUR MANDATE
================================================================================

The existing design is an explorer's FIELD JOURNAL: aged paper, india ink,
rubber stamps, washi tape, a torn flag swatch, a hand-drawn antique world map.
Palette is cream / near-black ink / one oxblood accent / brass / faded map teal.

YOU ARE NOT OBLIGED TO KEEP ANY OF IT.

This is a from-scratch redesign. You may discard the journal metaphor entirely
and take the game somewhere completely different, as long as you honour section 4
(the seven rules) and section 6 (the data you must display).

The original direction brief was "adventure / traveller / diary theme — creative
and bold". Treat that as the starting point for the feeling, not as a cage. If
you have a stronger idea, make the case for it and execute it with conviction.

Two things the current design got right and worth keeping in some form:
  - A limited palette with ONE hot accent reads as deliberate and confident.
  - Big scale contrast: large display type against small clean UI text.

One thing to beat: the current version is handsome but quiet. It could be bolder.

Non-negotiable regardless of direction:
  - MOBILE-FIRST. This is a daily-ritual game; assume 80%+ phone usage. Design
    390px first, then scale up. No horizontal scroll at 390px or 360px. Actions
    within thumb reach.
  - Zero layout shift when a new clue appends. The already-read clues must not
    move when Day N+1 arrives.
  - Honour prefers-reduced-motion: remove motion, don't just shorten it.
  - Text contrast must meet WCAG AA (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large). The build is gated
    on Lighthouse mobile accessibility >= 90.

4. SEVEN RULES THAT ARE NOT STYLING
================================================================================

These live in presentation files and look like arbitrary restrictions. They are
game-integrity rules. THREE OF THEM HAVE ALREADY SHIPPED AS BUGS ONCE AND HAD TO
BE FIXED. Please do not reintroduce them.

4.1  DAY 5 MUST NOT NAME THE LANGUAGE BEFORE THE GAME ENDS.
     Printing "Georgian" hands the player Georgia and makes the final Day a
     guaranteed solve. Recognising the language from its script IS the clue.
     Show the phrase. Reveal the language name only after resolution.

4.2  DAY 3 MUST NOT SHOW THE REAL FLAG BEFORE THE GAME ENDS.
     Showing Georgia's flag identifies the country instantly. Day 3 reveals the
     COLOUR SET only ("red and white — and no other colours"), never the layout.
     Represent colours abstractly. The actual flag may appear after resolution.

4.3  THE TRANSLATION MUST BE ABSENT FROM THE DOM, NOT CSS-HIDDEN.
     helpPhraseTranslation hidden with display:none is one devtools glance from
     spoiling the puzzle. Render it conditionally or not at all.

4.4  WRONG GUESSES SHOW DISTANCE BANDS, NEVER EXACT KILOMETRES.
     "Very close / Close / Far / Very far". Exact distances let a player
     trilaterate the answer from three guesses without reading a single clue.

4.5  NO CONTINUE BUTTON ON DAY 5.
     There is no Day 6. Day 5 offers only a final guess.

4.6  CLUES ACCUMULATE. NEVER REPLACE, NEVER CAROUSEL.
     The player must be able to re-read every earlier clue while deciding. The
     deduction depends on holding all revealed clues at once. A carousel or a
     "current clue only" view breaks the core mechanic.

4.7  ALL CLUE TEXT MUST BE REAL DOM TEXT.
     Never bake clue text into an image, a canvas, or an SVG <path>. It must be
     selectable and readable by screen readers. Decorative elements should be
     aria-hidden; the clue sentences must be in the accessibility tree.

4.8  (BONUS, LEGAL) THE GEONAMES ATTRIBUTION MUST STAY VISIBLE IN THE APP.
     "City data (c) GeoNames.org, licensed under CC BY 4.0" — CC BY 4.0 requires
     it in the product, not only in the README. You may restyle it freely, but it
     must remain rendered and legible. Same for the other credits in Footer.tsx.

ONE TECHNICAL TRAP
     If you use mix-blend-mode (multiply etc.) for ink-on-paper layering, the
     blended elements must sit inside a wrapper with `isolation: isolate`, and NO
     ancestor between them may have transform, filter, opacity < 1, or
     will-change. Those create a new stacking context and the blend fails
     SILENTLY — no error, just flat-looking colour.

     Also: SVG filters (feTurbulence/feDisplacementMap) are NOT GPU-accelerated.
     6-15ms per frame on desktop, worse on mobile, with a known Firefox perf bug
     and iOS Safari crash reports when animated over large areas. Use them only
     on small, static elements with a fixed seed. For full-bleed texture, use a
     pre-baked tileable image (the current one is 20KB).

5. THE SCREENS YOU NEED TO DESIGN
================================================================================

5.1  THE GAME (route "/")
     The main surface. Shows the revealed clue cards stacked in order, the
     current Day indicator, the puzzle number, the guess input with autocomplete,
     and the GUESS / CONTINUE actions.
     States: loading, Day 1 through Day 5, and wrong-guess marks persisting
     inline after the clue they were made on.

5.2  A WRONG GUESS
     Must read as a permanent record, not a transient toast. Shows the guessed
     city name and its distance BAND. It stays on screen for the rest of the game.

5.3  THE GUESS INPUT
     Text field + autocomplete dropdown over ~22,850 world cities. Critical
     behaviours to preserve:
       - Submit stays DISABLED until the player selects a real city from the
         dropdown (free text can never be submitted).
       - Dropdown labels disambiguate by country ("Paris, France" vs
         "Paris, United States").
       - Already-guessed cities appear marked and are not selectable.
       - Full keyboard support: arrow keys move, Enter selects, Escape dismisses.
         Use the ARIA combobox pattern (role=combobox, aria-expanded,
         aria-activedescendant). The accessibility score depends on this.
       - Input disabled while the city index is still loading.
       - The dropdown must not be hidden behind the mobile keyboard.

5.4  THE ENDGAME REVEAL
     The payoff screen. Must show:
       - A clear WON / LOST signal
       - The city and country
       - Day solved + number of wrong guesses + elapsed time
       - All five clues re-presented
       - The Day 5 phrase WITH its translation (now permitted)
       - The flag (now permitted)
       - A world map plotting the true city and the player's wrong guesses,
         joined by curved great-circle arcs
       - Source citations: population source, historical source (real links)
       - The share button and the countdown to tomorrow
     On a loss it must also show the player's final guess and its distance.

     ABOUT THE MAP: it is a vector world map drawn with d3-geo (not raster tiles,
     no API key, no satellite imagery). You can restyle its fills, strokes, ocean
     pattern and arc styling however you like. Please keep it vector and keep it
     tile-free — photographic tiles would fight almost any art direction and add
     a paid dependency.

     ON MOBILE: whatever two-up layout you design must collapse to a single
     scrolling column at 390px.

5.5  THE STATISTICS PANEL
     Games played, won, win %, current streak, longest streak, AVERAGE DAY SOLVED
     (this is the hero metric — give it primacy over streak and win rate), a
     distribution of solves across Day 1-5 plus Lost, and average solve time.
     Includes an export/import control for transferring progress between devices.
     ZERO STATE MATTERS: with no games played it must read 0 / "—" and must never
     render NaN.

5.6  THE SHARE ARTEFACT
     Produces a spoiler-free text block, currently:
         The Lost Explorer #124
         (five-cell strip, solved cell marked)
         Found on Day 3 · 2 wrong turns
         01:18
         Streak 1
     You may redesign the strip glyphs and the layout. ABSOLUTE RULE: the city
     and country name must never appear in the output.

5.7  THE "NO PUZZLE TODAY" STATE
     When no puzzle is published for the current date the game shows a resting
     state — the explorer is between journeys — plus the countdown. This is a
     real screen players will see, not an error state. Design it properly.

================================================================================
6. THE DATA YOU HAVE TO WORK WITH
================================================================================

Everything comes from one hook. Design against these exact shapes.

  useDailyGame() returns:
    status        'loading' | 'resting' | 'ready'
    clues         ServerDayClues | null   (only unlocked Days are present)
    countryCode   string | null
    resolution    ResolutionPayload | null   (null until the game ends)
    game          GameState | null
    startedAtMs   number
    resolvedAtMs  number | null
    submitGuess(candidate)
    continueDay()

  ServerDayClues — note every Day after day1 is OPTIONAL. It is absent from the
  payload until the player unlocks it. This is deliberate: it is what stops a
  player reading Day 5 on Day 1 from devtools. Design for progressive arrival.

    day1  { landscape: string; metroPopulationMillions: number }
    day2? { referenceCity, referenceCityCountry,
            referenceCityCoordinates {lat,lng}, distanceKm, recognisability }
    day3? { flagColours: string[] }
    day4? { mapYear: number; historicalEntity: string }
    day5? { language, languageCode, helpPhrase }

  ResolutionPayload — only after the game ends:
    city, country, countryCode, geonameId, coordinates {lat,lng},
    helpPhraseTranslation,
    populationSource { name, url, asOfYear },
    historicalSourceUrl

  GameState:
    dayKey, currentDay (1-5), guesses[], wrongGuesses[], status
    status is 'in_progress' | 'won' | 'lost'

  Statistics (computeStats):
    played, won, winPercent, currentStreak, longestStreak,
    averageDaySolved (number | null), daySolves {1,2,3,4,5},
    failed, averageSolveTimeMs (number | null)

  landscape is a fixed enum — you need a visual treatment for EVERY value or some
  future puzzle will render nothing:
    flat, hilly, mountainous, mountains-visible, mountains-surrounding,
    coastal, lakeside, river, dry, tropical, green-hills, desert, high-altitude

  The clue sentences themselves are generated by src/domain/clueText.ts. Use
  those functions; do not rewrite the wording. The templates are fixed by design
  so every city reads the same way.

================================================================================
7. TYPOGRAPHY — ONE REAL CONSTRAINT
================================================================================

Pick any fonts you like, but Day 5 renders the local language in its native
script. Across just the first six puzzles that includes Georgian, Vietnamese,
Catalan, Spanish, Icelandic and Bosnian — and the content set will grow to
include Japanese, Arabic, Cyrillic and Devanagari.

Most display and handwriting fonts cover Latin only. If your chosen face lacks a
script, that text renders as TOFU BOXES (empty rectangles). Tbilisi is the very
first puzzle and it is Georgian, so this is immediate, not hypothetical.

You need a per-script fallback stack, loaded per clue language rather than
bundling every script into the app. The current implementation uses Noto families
(Noto Sans Georgian, Noto Sans JP, Noto Naskh Arabic, Noto Sans Devanagari) and
code-splits them — a sound approach you can reuse or replace.

Also set lang= on the phrase element, for correct shaping and screen-reader
pronunciation.

Fonts are self-hosted via next/font/google (no runtime requests to Google).
Keep that. Current type payload is roughly 120-180KB; stay in that range.

9. HOW TO KNOW YOU HAVEN'T BROKEN ANYTHING
================================================================================

  bun test            expect 295 passing, 0 failing
  bunx tsc --noEmit   expect exit 0
  bun run build       expect exit 0
  bun run puzzle-lint expect 6/6 puzzles passed

If all four stay green you have not touched the engine. If bun test starts
failing you have edited something in src/domain, src/state or src/search — revert
that part.

Then verify by hand:
  - Play a full game on a 390px viewport. No horizontal scroll at any Day.
  - Confirm earlier clues stay visible and do not shift when a new one arrives.
  - On Day 3, confirm you cannot identify the country from what is shown.
  - On Day 5, confirm the language is not named and the translation is not in the
    DOM (check devtools, not just the screen).
  - Lose a game on purpose and confirm the reveal shows the answer, the map, the
    arcs and the sources.
  - Check the share text contains no city name.
  - Run Lighthouse mobile accessibility; it needs >= 90.

================================================================================
10. WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS GREAT
================================================================================

The mechanic's real pleasure is the moment the player thinks "wait — I think I
know where I am." The design should build toward that. Each new Day arriving
should feel like a small event, and the endgame should feel like a reward worth
coming back for tomorrow.

Things worth being bold about:
  - The arrival of a new clue. Right now it is tasteful. It could be a moment.
  - The win/lose beat. It should be unmistakable and a little theatrical.
  - The endgame map. It is the most visually interesting surface in the product
    and the main "I learned something" payoff. Let it breathe.
  - The share artefact. It is the game's only growth channel; it should be
    something people want to post.

Things to stay disciplined about:
  - Clue legibility beats atmosphere. A player must scan a clue in seconds.
  - Restraint in palette and type count reads as confidence.
  - It is a daily habit. It should feel calm and quick, not effortful. Whatever
    you design will be seen by the same person every single day — favour things
    that stay pleasant on the hundredth viewing over things that impress once.
