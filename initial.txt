# The Lost Explorer — Game Plan

## 1. Core concept

**The Lost Explorer** is a daily web-based geography deduction game.

Every day, all players are trying to identify the same mystery city.

The player receives one new clue per “day” of the explorer’s journey. The clues become progressively more useful.

The player can guess after each clue or continue to the next day.

The goal is to identify the city using as few clues as possible.

The game should have the simplicity and daily ritual of Wordle, while gradually improving the player’s mental map of the world.

---

# 2. Main design principles

The game should be:

- One new mystery city every day.
- The same puzzle for every player.
- Five clue stages maximum.
- Easy to understand without instructions after playing once.
- Difficult to solve early, but increasingly fair.
- Based on deduction rather than obscure trivia.
- Consistent in structure every day.
- Easy to generate from structured city data.
- Shareable without revealing the answer.
- Suitable for streaks, statistics, and leaderboards.

The major design rule is:

**Every city uses the same five clue categories and approximately the same wording.**

We should not write an entirely custom story for every city.

Instead, each daily puzzle is generated from structured data inserted into fixed narrative templates.

---

# 3. Game terminology

Do not call attempts “lives.”

Each clue stage is called a:

**Day**

Example:

- Day 1
- Day 2
- Day 3
- Day 4
- Day 5

This supports the narrative that the explorer has been lost for several days and is gradually discovering where they are.

---

# 4. Basic gameplay loop

Each daily puzzle starts with Day 1.

The player reads the clue and chooses between:

**GUESS**

or

**CONTINUE**

If they guess correctly:

- Puzzle ends.
- Their score is based primarily on which Day they solved it.
- Completion time can be used as a secondary ranking factor.
- Their streak and statistics update.

If they guess incorrectly:

- The guess is recorded.
- They proceed to the next Day.

If they choose Continue:

- They voluntarily sacrifice the possibility of a better Day score.
- The next clue appears.

On Day 5:

- The player receives the strongest clue.
- They make their final guess.
- If incorrect, they lose that day’s puzzle.

---

# 5. Fixed clue structure

Every mystery city uses the following exact clue progression.

## DAY 1 — Surroundings + population

The explorer looks around and describes:

1. The visible geography / environment.
2. The approximate population of the urban area.

The population should be explicitly shown numerically in millions.

Example template:

> I’ve had a look around. **[LANDSCAPE OBSERVATION].**
>
> This feels like a **[SIZE DESCRIPTION]** city — around **[POPULATION] million people** live here.

Examples:

> I’ve had a look around. The city is surrounded by hills, with mountains visible in the distance.
>
> This feels like a fairly busy city — around 1.2 million people live here.

Or:

> I’ve had a look around. The landscape seems mostly flat, and there’s a huge body of water nearby.
>
> This place feels enormous — around 14 million people live here.

### Population rules

Use **urban-area / metropolitan population**, not administrative city-boundary population where possible.

Population should be approximate and rounded.

Suggested descriptions:

- Under 0.2m → small
- 0.2–0.5m → fairly small
- 0.5–1m → medium-sized
- 1–2m → fairly busy
- 2–5m → big
- 5–10m → huge
- 10m+ → enormous

Exact wording can later be refined, but the numerical population should always remain visible.

Do not make population overly precise.

Examples:

- 0.18m → 0.2 million
- 0.74m → 0.7 million
- 1.26m → 1.3 million
- 4.7m → approximately 5 million
- 13.8m → approximately 14 million

### Day 1 geography data

Possible environmental observations include:

- Flat
- Hilly
- Mountainous
- Mountains visible in one direction
- Mountains surrounding the city
- Coast nearby
- Large lake nearby
- Major river visible / running through city
- Dry landscape
- Tropical vegetation
- Dense green hills
- Desert surroundings
- High-altitude appearance

The clue must remain broad enough that Day 1 solves are impressive.

---

## DAY 2 — Distance to another city

The explorer discovers a sign or other plausible reference to another known city.

Fixed style:

> I found a sign. It says **[REFERENCE CITY] is about [DISTANCE] km away.**

Example:

> I found a sign. It says Yerevan is about 170 km away.

Or:

> I found a sign. It says Zaragoza is about 250 km away.

Distances should usually be rounded.

Examples:

- ~100 km
- ~250 km
- ~500 km
- ~1,000 km

Avoid unnecessary precision such as 173.4 km.

### Important Day 2 design requirement

The reference city must be selected carefully.

It should:

- Narrow the search meaningfully.
- Not make the answer immediately obvious in most cases.
- Usually be recognizable to a reasonably geographically interested player.
- Not always be the nearest major city.
- Provide a useful spatial relationship without requiring exact map calculations.

This clue-selection logic should eventually be partially automated.

---

## DAY 3 — Flag colour

The explorer notices a flag but cannot make out its full design.

Fixed wording:

> I saw a flag in the distance. It definitely had **[COLOUR]** in it.

Only **one colour** should be revealed.

Examples:

Georgia:

> It definitely had red in it.

Estonia:

> It definitely had blue in it.

Brazil:

> It definitely had green in it.

Ukraine:

> It definitely had yellow in it.

### Flag colour selection rule

Prefer a colour that provides information without immediately identifying the country.

Where multiple colours are available, generally choose the less distinctive / less revealing option.

The flag clue should narrow the possibilities, not solve the puzzle by itself.

---

## DAY 4 — Historical geography

This introduces the history element.

The explorer finds an old map or equivalent historical clue.

Fixed construction:

> I found an old map dated **[YEAR]**. Apparently this place was part of **[HISTORICAL STATE / EMPIRE / POLITICAL ENTITY]** at the time.

Examples:

Tbilisi:

> I found an old map dated 1900. Apparently this place was part of the Russian Empire at the time.

Sarajevo:

> I found an old map dated 1980. Apparently this place was part of Yugoslavia at the time.

Prague:

> I found an old map dated 1910. Apparently this place was part of Austria-Hungary at the time.

Hanoi:

> I found an old map dated 1900. Apparently this place was part of French Indochina at the time.

### Historical clue rules

The year can differ for each city.

Choose a year that creates an interesting but useful clue.

The clue should teach historical geography rather than rely on random trivia.

Prefer:

- Historical states
- Empires
- Former federations
- Colonial territories
- Kingdoms
- Former national borders

Avoid clues requiring knowledge of very obscure individual historical events unless intentionally used for a harder mode later.

---

## DAY 5 — Local language

This is the final and strongest clue.

A local notices that the explorer is lost.

Fixed construction:

> Someone finally noticed I was lost. They looked at me and said:
>
> **“[LOCAL LANGUAGE VERSION OF ‘DO YOU NEED HELP?’]”**
>
> **FINAL GUESS**

Do not display an English translation before the player guesses.

Examples:

Catalan:

> “Necessites ajuda?”

Estonian:

> “Kas vajate abi?”

Georgian:

> “დახმარება ხომ არ გჭირდება?”

Japanese:

> “助けが必要ですか？”

The phrase should always mean approximately:

- Do you need help?
- Can I help you?
- Are you lost?

Prefer one standardized meaning across all languages.

Language is intentionally kept until Day 5 because it is frequently one of the strongest possible geographic clues.

Day 5 should feel like a recognizable recurring moment in the game.

---

# 6. Clue strength progression

The intended information curve is:

### Day 1
Broadly narrows the type and size of city.

### Day 2
Creates a strong geographic constraint.

### Day 3
Adds likely national / regional information.

### Day 4
Adds historical-political context.

### Day 5
Usually makes the country or linguistic region highly identifiable.

The player should increasingly feel:

“I think I know where I am.”

The puzzle should not depend on one obscure fact.

Correct solving should come from combining clues.

---

# 7. Guessing rules

Players may guess once after each revealed Day.

They may also choose to continue without guessing.

Wrong guesses advance the player to the next Day.

The same city cannot be guessed twice.

Autocomplete / search should help normalize:

- Alternate city spellings
- Accents
- Local names
- English names
- Historical names where appropriate

Example:

- München → Munich
- Firenze → Florence
- København → Copenhagen

Potential policy for historical city names should be decided later.

---

# 8. Wrong-guess feedback

Wrong guesses should provide only limited information.

Recommended starting version:

> ❌ Yerevan  
> About 170 km away.

Do **not** initially provide direction.

Direction + distance could overpower the designed clue progression and make the game resemble existing city-direction games too closely.

Potential distance bands instead of exact distance:

- Very close — under 250 km
- Close — under 750 km
- Far — under 2,000 km
- Very far — over 2,000 km

Exact-distance vs distance-band feedback should be playtested.

---

# 9. City selection

Do not make every answer a national capital.

Otherwise experienced players will simply cycle through capitals.

Include:

- Capitals
- Major cities
- Medium-sized internationally relevant cities
- Historic cities
- Regional centres
- Culturally important cities

Potential examples:

- Naples
- Ulaanbaatar
- Zanzibar City
- Cusco
- Sarajevo
- Jaipur
- Reykjavík
- Tbilisi
- Barcelona
- Tallinn
- Hanoi
- Prague

The answer pool should still avoid locations so obscure that the game becomes impossible for normal players.

An eventual difficulty classification may be useful:

- Easy
- Standard
- Hard

But difficulty should not necessarily be shown before the puzzle.

---

# 10. Scoring

Primary score should be based on which Day the player solves the city.

Example:

- Day 1 = best
- Day 2 = excellent
- Day 3 = good
- Day 4 = acceptable
- Day 5 = survived
- Failure = lost

Do not allow speed to dominate the game.

Time can be used as a leaderboard tiebreaker between players who solved on the same Day.

Potential scoring models can be tested later.

The core metric users should care about is:

**Average Day solved**

Example player profile:

- Average solve: Day 2.8
- Best solve: Day 1
- Current streak: 18
- Win rate: 93%

---

# 11. End-of-game result

On success:

> FOUND
>
> You found yourself in **Tbilisi, Georgia**
>
> Solved on Day 3
>
> Time: 01:18

On failure:

> LOST
>
> The explorer was in **Montevideo, Uruguay**
>
> Your final guess: Córdoba, Argentina
>
> Distance: 1,057 km

After the puzzle ends, it is acceptable to reveal more information.

Possible post-game content:

- City on a map
- All five clues
- Player’s guesses plotted geographically
- Actual distance of each guess
- Population source
- Historical clue explanation
- Flag
- Translation of the language clue
- Short educational fact about the city

This reinforces the learning aspect.

---

# 12. Sharing

Shared results must not reveal the answer.

Possible format:

The Lost Explorer #147

🟩 Day 3  
⏱ 01:18  
🔥 12

Or use a five-stage visual:

⬜⬜🟩⬛⬛

Meaning the city was solved on Day 3.

More distinctive symbols can be designed later.

Sharing should show:

- Puzzle number
- Day solved
- Time if desired
- Streak

Never show city name.

# 15. Daily puzzle data model

Each city should have structured data similar to:

```json
{
  "city": "Tbilisi",
  "country": "Georgia",
  "accepted_names": ["Tbilisi"],
  "coordinates": {
    "lat": 41.7151,
    "lng": 44.8271
  },
  "urban_population_millions": 1.2,
  "population_description": "fairly busy",
  "day1_environment": "The city is surrounded by hills, with mountains visible in the distance.",
  "day2_reference_city": "Yerevan",
  "day2_distance_km": 170,
  "day3_flag_colour": "red",
  "day4_year": 1900,
  "day4_historical_entity": "the Russian Empire",
  "day5_language": "Georgian",
  "day5_help_phrase": "დახმარება ხომ არ გჭირდება?"
}
```

Additional fields should later store:

- Sources
- Urban population year
- Historical-source verification
- Language variants
- Flag data
- Difficulty estimate
- Time zone
- Country code
- City aliases

---

# 16. Puzzle generation workflow

The goal is that making a daily puzzle becomes mostly data entry / validation rather than creative writing.

Proposed workflow:

### Step 1
Select mystery city.

### Step 2
Retrieve / verify:

- Coordinates
- Urban population
- Physical geography
- National flag colours
- Historical political geography
- Local language

### Step 3
Select a useful Day 2 reference city and calculate approximate distance.

### Step 4
Select the least revealing reasonable flag colour.

### Step 5
Choose a historically useful year and political entity.

### Step 6
Generate / verify the standardized “Do you need help?” phrase in an appropriate local language.

### Step 7
Run puzzle-quality checks.

### Step 8
Publish as the next daily puzzle.

Most clue text should come from templates rather than being rewritten manually.

---

# 17. Quality-control checks

Before publishing a city, test:

### Day 1
- Is the population reasonably current?
- Are we consistently using urban-area / metropolitan population?
- Is the environmental clue accurate?
- Is it broad enough?

### Day 2
- Is the reference city well known enough?
- Is the distance accurate?
- Does this clue accidentally reveal the answer immediately?

### Day 3
- Is the colour genuinely on the national flag?
- Is another colour less revealing?
- Does this combine fairly with previous clues?

### Day 4
- Was the city genuinely part of the named entity in the stated year?
- Is the historical wording politically and historically accurate?
- Is the clue understandable to a general player?

### Day 5
- Is the phrase grammatically natural?
- Is the chosen language actually used locally?
- If several languages are common, is the selected language fair?
- Does the phrase accidentally contain the city/country name?

### Overall
- Could several plausible cities still fit before Day 5?
- Does each clue materially narrow the possibilities?
- Is the final answer realistically solvable?
- Is one clue disproportionately powerful?

---

# 18. Important edge cases

## Multilingual cities

Cities may have several widely spoken languages.

We need a policy.

Potential approach:

Use the locally dominant language most likely to be heard in ordinary street interaction.

For genuinely multilingual cities, this must be manually reviewed.

Examples requiring care:

- Brussels
- Montreal
- Barcelona
- Singapore
- Luxembourg
- multilingual African cities

---

## City population definitions

Population numbers vary hugely depending on:

- City proper
- Urban area
- Metropolitan area

The game should define one consistent methodology.

Preferred:

**Urban-area population where reliable data exists.**

Display rounded approximate numbers to reduce disputes.

---

## Historic borders

Historical territorial ownership can be politically sensitive or complicated.

Use neutral wording:

> “This place was part of [entity] at the time.”

Avoid implying modern territorial claims.

Every Day 4 clue should be fact-checked.

---

## Flag ambiguity

Some countries have extremely distinctive colour combinations.

Since only one colour is shown, select the clue that best preserves difficulty.

---

# 19. Tone and writing style

The explorer should sound conversational and lightly humorous, but the writing should not distract from the deduction.

Good:

> I saw a flag in the distance. It definitely had red in it.

Good:

> Someone finally noticed I was lost.

Avoid overly elaborate prose.

The player should be able to scan each clue in a few seconds.

The repeated structure should become familiar and comforting.

---

# 20. Recommended MVP

Build the smallest playable version first.

MVP should contain:

- One daily city
- Five fixed clue stages
- Guess / Continue
- City autocomplete
- Correct / incorrect validation
- Distance calculation for guesses
- Daily reset
- Streak
- Basic statistics
- Spoiler-free share result

Do not initially prioritize:

- Friends system
- Complex leaderboards
- Achievements
- Audio pronunciation
- Multiple game modes
- Accounts unless needed for syncing
- Elaborate animations

The most important thing to test is whether the five-clue progression is genuinely fun.

Before building a large city database, create approximately 20 manually curated puzzles.

Use geographically varied cities.

Suggested test cities:

- Barcelona

- Sarajevo

- Hanoi
- Prague

- Montevideo

The desired solve distribution should probably peak around **Day 3**, not Day 1 or Day 5.

---

# 22. Questions still to resolve through testing

These should not block the MVP.

1. Should wrong guesses reveal exact distance or only distance bands?
2. Should players have one guess per Day or unlimited guesses with penalties?
3. How should scoring work numerically?
4. Should completion time affect score or only leaderboard ties?
5. What exact population dataset should be used?
6. How should multilingual cities choose the Day 5 language?
7. Should capitals appear less frequently than other cities?
8. Should Sunday / weekend puzzles be easier?
9. Should players be allowed to play previous missed puzzles?
10. Should post-game maps show the route between guesses?
11. Should Day 5 eventually include optional audio pronunciation?
12. Should difficulty be manually assigned or calculated from player results?

---

# 23. Core product identity

The game's strongest idea is not simply:

**“Guess a city.”**

It is:

**“You are lost somewhere in the world. Each day you discover one more thing about where you are.”**

The five clues should gradually move the player through:

**physical geography → relative geography → national identity → historical geography → language**

That progression is the central mechanic and should remain consistent across every puzzle.
